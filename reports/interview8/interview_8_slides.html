01 / THE PROBLEM

Flow through a random network

A simplified fracture network

25 × 25 nodes; 1,200 potential links.

A fixed driving force

Left head = 1; right head = 0. No external flow at top or bottom.

What do we want to predict?

Total flow and effective conductance: G_eff = Q_total / Δh.

Dimensionless model • Illustration uses 11 × 11 nodes for clarity.1
02 / MODEL AND SOLUTION

From uncertain links to a flow prediction

01 Connectivity

One global p per network:
p ∼ Uniform(0.5 − δ, 0.5 + δ).
Each link is active with probability p.

02 Link conductance

Active links: ln(g) ∼ Normal(0, σ²).
Inactive links: g = 0.
Larger σ means greater heterogeneity.

03 Solve node heads

Find spanning components.
Enforce flow balance at free nodes:
Σⱼ gᵢⱼ(hᵢ − hⱼ) = 0.

04 Compute the output

Solve the sparse linear system for all unknown heads together.
Then calculate link flows and G_eff.

δ controls uncertainty in connectivity; σ controls spread in log-conductance.2

04 / ANALYTICAL BENCHMARKS

Does the solver reproduce known answers?

Uniform grid

Exact: G = g n / (n − 1).
Linear head profile also verified.
4 sizes × 3 conductances.

Series and parallel paths

Series: G = 1 / Σ(1/g).
Parallel: branch conductances add.
Both agree with analytical answers.

Disconnected network

A complete vertical cut removes
every spanning path.
Computed conductance: exactly zero.

Scaling and monotonicity

G(3g) = 3G(g).
Increasing edge conductances
never reduced G in 12 test cases.

39 recorded benchmark / invariant checks: all passed • Original automated suite: 24 tests.4
05 / UNCERTAINTY ANALYSIS

How do uncertainties change the mean flow?

Wider connectivity uncertainty

At σ = 1, mean G increases
0.0183 → 0.0992 as δ: 0 → 0.25.

More heterogeneous conductance

At fixed δ, mean G decreases
as σ increases in these scenarios.

Why this happens

Connectivity has a nonlinear response.
Weak links can become bottlenecks.

9 scenarios × 10,000 realizations • Error bars: Monte Carlo 95% confidence intervals.5

08 / DISCUSSION

What we learned — and how to extend the model

01 Main finding: connectivity

Connection structure and its interactions
dominate output variability
over the selected input ranges.

02 Main finding: distribution

Mean conductance is not enough.
Report zero-flow probability,
conditional flow and an upper quantile.

03 Model limitations

Regular 2D geometry; steady linear flow.
Exploratory priors; finite-size effects.
No geological field calibration.

04 Toward realistic fractured rocks

Use 3D discrete fracture networks,
aperture-based conductance and correlation.
Calibrate with head, flow or tracer data.

Numerical verification establishes implementation correctness; it does not establish field prediction accuracy.8
