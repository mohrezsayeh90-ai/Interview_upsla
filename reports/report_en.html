Uncertainty and sensitivity of flow in a 2D random network

Finding. Connectivity controls both the existence and magnitude of flow. Broadening the uncertain connectivity distribution increases mean conductance strongly, while the probability of a spanning path stays close to one half. Conductance heterogeneity changes flow magnitude, with bottlenecks dominating near the connectivity threshold.

At σ=1, increasing δ from 0 to 0.25 changes mean effective conductance from 0.0183 to 0.0992 (5.44×). In the latter case, 48.62% of realizations carry no flow. These results come from 248,188 solver calls, including 90,000 main uncertainty realizations.

Model and scope

A square bond lattice has n=25 nodes per side (625 nodes; 1,200 potential links). All quantities are dimensionless. Each realization samples one global p; links are independent conditional on that p. The left/right hydraulic heads are 1/0; the top/bottom boundaries have no exterior links and hence no imposed flux.

p = 0.5 + δU, U ∼ Uniform(−1,1)
gₑ = 1[Wₑ < p] exp(σZₑ), Wₑ ∼ Uniform(0,1), Zₑ ∼ Normal(0,1)
Qᵢⱼ = gᵢⱼ(hᵢ−hⱼ), ΣⱼQᵢⱼ = 0, G_eff = Q_total / Δh = Q_total

1 · Numerical method and verification

Build the weighted graph Laplacian L. Connected-component analysis identifies clusters touching both fixed-head boundaries. Solve only free nodes in these clusters using SciPy’s sparse direct solver:

L_FF h_F = −L_FB h_B

Clusters touching only one boundary have constant head and zero flow. Interior floating clusters have arbitrary absolute head (stored as NaN) and zero flow; they are excluded from the singular linear system. Total inlet flux is evaluated on spanning clusters. This avoids artificial leakage or regularization.

3 · Distribution and connectivity transition

For the reference scenario, the atom at zero is 0.4862; the mean, median and upper tail are far apart. Report P(G=0) and positive-flow statistics separately. The density plot describes log₁₀(G) conditional on positive flow, not the density of G. The CDF panel focuses on 0≤G≤0.75; full values are retained in the raw data.

Increasing δ leaves E[p]=0.5, but spreads realizations below and above the connectivity transition. The nonlinear flow response gives high-p networks substantial flow, while low-p networks remain at zero. This explains the increased mean without a comparable increase in spanning probability; it is an empirical result over these ranges, not a general convexity theorem.

Unconditional links are correlated because they share p: for different edge indicators Iₑ,I𝒻, Cov(Iₑ,I𝒻)=Var(p)=δ²/3. Sampling a separate p for every edge would remove this mechanism and implement a different model.

4 · Global sensitivity with stochastic inputs

Use grouped variance-based Sobol analysis [1], which captures threshold behavior and interactions. Expand the stochastic simulator into five independent input groups: δ∼U(0,0.25), σ∼U(0,2), U∼U(−1,1), topology field W (1,200 uniforms), and conductance field Z (1,200 normals). This avoids incorrectly treating δ and the realized p as independent inputs.

Sᵢ = Var(E[Y | Xᵢ]) / Var(Y)
Sᵀᵢ = E[Var(Y | X_−ᵢ)] / Var(Y)
Ŝᵢ = 1 − mean[(Y_B−Y_ABᵢ)²]/(2V), Ŝᵀᵢ = mean[(Y_A−Y_ABᵢ)²]/(2V)

A and B are independent grouped input samples; ABᵢ replaces group i of A with B. V is the pooled sample variance of Y_A and Y_B. Use 16,384 independent base pairs (114,688 evaluations), with 1,000 paired-row bootstrap resamples for percentile 95% intervals.

U dominates; δ has a much larger total than first-order effect, indicating interactions, principally with the connectivity draw. About 30.7% of variance is assigned to interactions by the sum of estimated first-order indices. Total indices overlap and must not be added as independent percentages. σ and Z have exactly zero total effect on spanning because every active conductance is positive. Small spurious first-order estimates for known inactive factors are sampling error; raw estimates are preserved. Rankings depend on the chosen priors and latent parameterization and concern all realizations, including zero-flow networks.

5 · Convergence, finite size and normalization

The final relative 95% Monte Carlo half-width of the mean is 2.19–2.91% across the nine scenarios. At p=0.5 (δ=0, σ=1), mean G decreases from 0.0335 at n=15 to 0.0183 at n=25 and 0.0107 at n=41. Finite-size effects remain material near the threshold. Results cannot be advertised as a size-independent continuum prediction. Sobol prefix convergence is saved in figure 08 and CSV; the leading ranking is resolved, while tiny first-order terms remain uncertain.

In the specified model E[g | active]=exp(σ²/2), equal to 1, 1.6487 and 7.3891. Nevertheless mean G falls as σ increases in the studied scenarios: for δ=0.25, from 0.1222 to 0.0639. Weak links on series paths constrain flow. To isolate variability at fixed mean link conductance, set g*=g exp(−σ²/2); linearity gives G*=G exp(−σ²/2) exactly. This is a supplementary control and does not replace the assigned distribution.

6 · Interpretation, limitations and extensions

Main findings

  • Connectivity and its interactions dominate global output variance over the declared ranges. Characterizing connectivity has more value here than measuring every individual link conductance precisely.
  • The mean alone hides a near-50% probability of no flow and a highly variable positive component. A useful flow forecast includes connection probability, conditional flow and an upper quantile.
  • Broader uncertain connectivity increases mean flow at fixed E[p], while greater log-conductance spread reduces mean flow in this study. Both are range- and model-dependent findings.

Limitations

  • Regular, isotropic, two-dimensional bond geometry; no fracture length/orientation distributions, intersections, aperture physics or spatially correlated fields conditional on p.
  • Steady, single-phase, linear hydraulic conductance; no storage, transport, transient pressure, matrix flow, multiphase processes or geomechanical feedback.
  • Ranges are exploratory, not calibrated priors. The model is dimensionless and has not been validated against field data. Analytical and invariant checks establish implementation correctness, not geological predictive accuracy.
  • Finite-size dependence is significant. Near-critical paths, rare tails and strongly heterogeneous conductances need more sampling and conditioning checks if ranges are extended.

Extensions to fractured rocks

Generate discrete fracture networks in 2D/3D with measured fracture geometry and intersection statistics; link conductance to aperture and the cubic law with consistent units; introduce anisotropy and spatial correlation; calibrate against heads, flow or tracer data. Include matrix exchange and transient transport, then propagate parameter and structural uncertainty. Surrogates could accelerate these richer models after validating their errors near the connectivity transition; they are unnecessary for this inexpensive lattice study.
